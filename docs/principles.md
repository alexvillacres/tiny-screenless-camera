# Design Principles

## 1. Preserve the moment without interrupting it

The core interaction is one physical press. Any feature that asks for attention at capture time must justify its existence.

## 2. Discovery happens later

No screen, immediate preview, or automatic phone delivery. Delay is part of the product, not a technical shortcoming.

## 3. Simple must still be trustworthy

Lo-fi images are welcome; missed or silently failed captures are not. The camera should give subtle, unambiguous confirmation only after a file is safely saved.

## 4. Character matters more than specifications

Resolution, sharpness, and dynamic range are not primary goals. Early-digital artifacts, imperfect framing, and modest sensors may contribute to the camera's identity.

## 5. Remove choices at capture time

Avoid modes, zoom, filters, menus, and exposure controls. Creative decisions can happen through how the object is carried, pointed, and used.

## 6. Make it easy to carry

The camera only removes friction if it is available. Pocket, keychain, or small clip use should guide size, durability, wake time, and battery decisions.

## 7. Do not add artificial scarcity without evidence

Delayed viewing provides the desired separation from the moment. A simulated 24- or 36-exposure limit is not part of the current concept.

## 8. Earn complexity in stages

First make capture reliable over USB power. Then add portability. Wireless transfer, a phone experience, and custom electronics are later experiments—not prerequisites.
