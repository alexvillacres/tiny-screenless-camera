# Product Vision

## Vision

Create a tiny, screenless digital camera that lets a person preserve a candid memory without stepping out of the moment to manage a device.

## The problem

Phone photography is capable but attention-heavy. Taking a phone out, opening its camera, framing through a display, and reviewing the result introduces friction and invites self-editing. The photographer can become an observer of the moment instead of a participant in it.

Film cameras offer a compelling alternative: a simple physical gesture followed by delayed discovery. But film, processing, and specialist knowledge add recurring cost and inconvenience.

## The promise

One press should be enough to remember something.

The camera should:

- be present when a phone would feel intrusive;
- capture without a preview, retake loop, or immediate judgment;
- create photographs with lo-fi early-digital character;
- defer viewing until the owner intentionally retrieves the images;
- feel dependable despite its deliberate simplicity.

## What success feels like

The owner carries the camera without planning to “go take photos.” When something feels worth remembering, they point, press, notice a quiet confirmation, and return their attention to the moment. Later, viewing the photographs feels like finding memories rather than checking output.

## Audience

The first user is the project creator. The project may eventually appeal to people who value presence, candid memories, tactile objects, playful constraints, and the character of imperfect images.

## Product boundaries

This is not:

- a replacement for a modern phone or dedicated high-quality camera;
- a live-view or precision-composition tool;
- a social sharing device;
- a camera that encourages immediate review;
- an attempt to simulate every ritual or limitation of film.
